The finding, and the confound that nearly ate it

On raw pool counts the multi-agent panel looks like the winner. Arm B (five Haiku agents) reaches 30.40 distinct ideas against arm A's 27.10, at slightly lower cost.

But B produced 60 candidates to A's 30. Since distinct_k rises with pool size, that comparison is close to meaningless. Rarefying both arms down to a matched 27 candidates reverses the ordering — and restricting both to round-1 output only, so the round counts match too, leaves the reversal intact.

What this does not show. Arm A is Sonnet and arm B is Haiku, so panel-versus-solo is still fully confounded with Sonnet-versus-Haiku. Quantity is not quality — and quality is the part we cannot measure

In human ideation, more ideas produce better ideas only through narrowing. So the honest question is whether combining models and providers yields more diverse or better ideas — and whether we can even tell.

Can we identify the best ideas? No.

Three independent reasons, all already on the record:

  1. “Best” has no single definition here, by design. §6.3 forbids collapsing novelty and feasibility into one score, and the judge enforces it at write time with assertAxesNotCollapsed.
  2. The answer key cannot validate a judge. Human–human split-half balanced accuracy on the Si et al. slice is 0.5534, CI [0.4483, 0.6702] — it does not exclude chance. Expert humans do not agree with each other above chance on that slice, so no LLM judge can be validated against it. Idea-level metrics are exploratory by construction, not pending a gate that might later pass.
  3. Zero registered hypotheses are about quality. H1–H4 measure distinct_k; H5 measures judge validity. The study measures diversity per dollar. That is its scope.

Issue #52's crowdsourced human rating panel is the only route to a quality answer that doesn't depend on an unvalidatable judge. It is registered as exploratory, moscow:could, unscoped and unfunded — a fund-or-drop decision.

The sharpest finding — evals/harness/provider.mjs
The convergence stage is never run.

ideate-core is a divergent→convergent engine: S3 convergence (embedding dedup, clustering, split-axis scoring, cross-cluster selection) and S4 the evaluate→regenerate loop are where more ideas would become better ideas. The harness calls ideateCore() with complete, buildRound1Prompt, buildRound2Prompt, agents and maxRounds — and passes neither deps.convergence nor deps.feedback, so that branch never fires. The study measures the raw divergent pool only. This is scope, not a defect, but extending it is a registration amendment.

Limitations to say out loud

  1. Brief variance swamps arm variance. Arm B scores 40.4 on biz-01 and 20.4 on biz-02; the pilot ranges from 48.8 on sci-03 to 25.5 on biz-04. Which brief you ask matters roughly twice as much as which arm answers.
  2. Two briefs cannot support brief-as-random-effect. The mixed model fit, but the robustness check refused outright: fewer clusters (2, brief) than parameters (2). No confidence interval from this store is trustworthy.
  3. Idea-level metrics are exploratory by construction while the judge validation gate is uncleared — regardless of the judge rows sitting on disk.
  4. H5, self-preference bias, is structurally unidentifiable without arm G. With every generator on one provider, the bias column is collinear with the judge-provider dummy.
  5. Cost separation is absent. Distinct ideas per dollar: A 683 [657, 713], B 796 [534, 1058]. The intervals overlap; no ranking is supported. On a rarefied basis A's efficiency advantage would widen.
  6. Pilot and main-store dollars are not comparable and are deliberately not combined here — the main store billed at single-call rates, the pilot through the batch provider.
