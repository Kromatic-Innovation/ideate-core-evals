Full-pool `distinct_k` looks like the panel wins:

**But B got there with twice the candidates.** Rarefying both arms to a matched 27 candidates (repo's own `rarefaction.mjs`, r=1000, pinned seed, registered threshold):

| Arm | Full-pool | **Rarefied to 27** |
|---|---|---|
| A (solo Sonnet) | 27.10 | **24.57** (sd 1.65) |
| B (5× Haiku panel) | 30.40 | **17.13** (sd 4.45) |

The ordering **reverses**. At equal volume the single Sonnet call is markedly more diverse than the five-Haiku panel, and far more consistent. B's full-pool edge is bought entirely with volume, at roughly equal cost.

### The rounds confound — checked and eliminated

Arm A is one round (30 candidates); arm B is two rounds that *append* (30 + 30 = 60), with round 2 run under pool-sharing. So B's rarefied draw was half round-2 output produced with sight of round 1 — which would mechanically depress its diversity. Restricting **both arms to round-1 candidates only** and rarefying to 27 (pool arrays verified index-aligned to `candidates`, A `roundsRun`=1, B `roundsRun`=2):

| Arm | Rarefied to 27, **round 1 only** |
|---|---|
| A (solo Sonnet, 30 cand) | **24.57** (sd 1.65) |
| B (5× Haiku panel, 30 cand) | **16.42** (sd 4.18) |

The gap is unchanged. **The reversal is not a rounds artifact** — it holds in a clean like-for-like comparison: same volume, same round count, panel vs solo.

A secondary finding falls out of this: round 2 doubled B's candidates (30 → 60) and moved rarefied `distinct_k` only 16.42 → 17.13. **The second round bought volume, not variety.**

**What this still does NOT show:** arm A is Sonnet and arm B is Haiku, so panel-vs-solo remains confounded with Sonnet-vs-Haiku. This cannot distinguish "the panel doesn't help" from "Haiku is less diverse than Sonnet." **Arm C (homogeneous Sonnet panel) is the cell that separates them, and it has not been run.** Do not state this as a refutation of the multi-agent claim on Thursday — state it as a signal that makes running C urgent.

## Quantity vs quality — what this study can and cannot measure

**Is there a good way to determine which ideas are BEST? No.** Three independent reasons, all already on the record:

1. **"Best" has no single definition here, by design.** §6.3 forbids collapsing novelty and feasibility into one score (Rietzschel split-axis), and `evals/judge/prompt.mjs` enforces it at write time with `assertAxesNotCollapsed`.
2. **The answer key cannot validate a judge.** Appendix B item 7: human-human split-half balanced accuracy on the Si et al. slice is **0.5534, 95% CI [0.4483, 0.6702] — it does not exclude chance (0.50)**. Expert humans do not agree with each other above chance on that slice, so no LLM judge can be validated against it. Idea-level metrics are exploratory *by construction*, not pending a gate that might later pass.
3. **Zero registered hypotheses are about quality.** All five (H1–H4 on `distinct_k`, H5 on judge validity) measure diversity or instrument validity. The study measures **diversity per dollar**. That is its registered scope.

The only real path to a quality answer is issue #52's crowdsourced (Prolific) human rating panel — registered as exploratory, `moscow:could`, unscoped and unfunded. It is a fund-or-drop decision.

### The stance confound — and it is not a footnote

`arms.config.json` claims "the ONLY thing varying between arms is model assignment." **That claim is false for arm A versus any panel arm.**

| | Arm A | Arm B |
|---|---|---|
| Agents | 1 | 5 |
| Stances | PRAGMATIST only | PRAGMATIST, CONTRARIAN, DOMAIN-EXPERT, OUTSIDER-ANALOGY, VISIONARY |
| Temperature | 0.4 | 0.4 – 1.0 |
| Rounds | 1 | 2 |

Arm A's sole agent is instructed to *"prefer clarity and feasibility over cleverness."* So A varies in stance, temperature, rounds **and** model. This cuts both ways, and both directions matter:

- **On diversity, the handicap runs against the finding.** A wins rarefied `distinct_k` *despite* being the narrowest stance at the lowest temperature. That strengthens the diversity result.
- **On originality, the handicap explains the finding.** A is told to be pragmatic, and scores exactly like it.

Exploratory judged-originality figures, rarefied to 27 candidates (r=1000, pinned seed), judge `gpt-5.6-terra` — the only judge that scored both arms:

| | Arm A | Arm B |
|---|---|---|
| Max originality (round 1, matched volume) | 3.64 | 6.98 |
| Top-5 mean originality (round 1) | 3.10 | 6.31 |
| Mean feasibility (full pool) | 9.05 | 6.92 |

The originality gap is **not** a volume artifact — it survives rarefaction. But it is what the stances instructed, so it is not evidence about panels. Note also that arm A′ does not fix this: it varies personas *and* model relative to A. **The clean solo control — arm A with the panel's stance set, or a stance-neutral solo — does not exist in the registered grid.** That is a registration question, not something to patch quietly.

### The convergence gap — the sharpest finding

In human ideation, quantity improves quality *only through narrowing*. ideate-core has that narrowing stage: S3 convergence (embedding dedup + clustering + split-axis scoring + cross-cluster selection) and S4 the evaluate→regenerate feedback loop.

**The study never runs either.** `evals/harness/provider.mjs` calls `ideateCore()` with `complete`, `buildRound1Prompt`, `buildRound2Prompt`, `agents`, `maxRounds` — and passes neither `deps.convergence` nor `deps.feedback`, so `lib/ideate-core.mjs`'s `if (deps.convergence)` branch never fires.

So the study measures the **raw divergent pool** of a divergent→convergent engine. The mechanism by which more ideas would become better ideas is exactly the half that is untested. This is scope, not a defect — but extending it is a registration amendment, and it is the decision most worth making deliberately.

## Limitations to say out loud

1. **Brief variance swamps arm variance.** Arm B: biz-01 40.4 vs biz-02 20.4. Pilot: sci-03 48.8 vs biz-04 25.5. The brief matters roughly twice as much as the arm across everything measured.
2. **n=2 briefs in the main store cannot support brief-as-random-effect.** The mixed model fit at rung R0 but the CR2 robustness check refused outright: *"fewer clusters (2, brief) than parameters (2)."* Any CI from this store is not trustworthy.
3. **Idea-level metrics are exploratory by construction** (Appendix B item 7) because judge validation (#16) has not cleared — regardless of the judge rows on disk.
4. **H5 (self-preference bias) is structurally unidentifiable** without arm G — the bias column is collinear with the judge-provider dummy when every generator is Anthropic.
5. **Cost separation is absent.** distinct_k/dollar: A 683 [657, 713], B 796 [534, 1058]. Overlapping — no ranking is supported. (These are full-pool figures; on a rarefied basis A's cost-efficiency advantage would widen.)
6. **Main-store and pilot dollars are not comparable and are deliberately not combined here.** `results/` rows are `billing_mode: "api"`; the pilot ran through the batch provider at a different pricing regime.
