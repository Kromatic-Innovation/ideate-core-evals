Per-cell metrics: [`study1-stage1b-cells.csv`](study1-stage1b-cells.csv).

## Headline: personas do a great deal, and the panel still does not beat one call

Registered contrasts, rarefied to the minimum pool present (N = 45, Appendix C item 2), brief as a random effect, Holm-Bonferroni at m = 2:

| ID | Contrast | Estimate | 95% CI | Holm p | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **S1b-1** | `S1B-PANEL` − `S1B-SOLO60` | **−1.77** | [−3.55, **+0.02**] | 0.052 | not significant |
| **S1b-2** | `S1B-PANEL` − `S1B-APRIME` | **+14.91** | [+13.12, +16.69] | < 0.001 | **significant** |

Arm means at the matched pool of 45:

| Arm | Rarefied `distinct_k` | Full-pool `distinct_k` | Model calls per cell |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `S1B-SOLO60` | **40.18** | 53.25 | **1** |
| `S1B-PANEL` | 38.42 | 47.51 | **10** |
| `S1B-APRIME` | 23.51 | 27.38 | 10 |

Exploratory (BH lane): `S1B-APRIME` − `S1B-SOLO60` = **−16.67**.

**Read S1b-1's confidence interval, not its p-value.** The point estimate is negative and the upper bound is **+0.02** — this run excludes any panel advantage above a fortieth of one idea, at ten times the model calls. That is not "we failed to detect an effect"; it is an effect bounded at zero from a direction the registration did not enumerate.

**S1b-2 says the persona lever is real and large.** Five agents under *identical* personas produce 23.5 distinct ideas where five differentiated agents produce 38.4 — and where **one** solo call produces 40.2. So persona engineering recovers most of a large deficit that the panel structure itself creates. It does not clear the benchmark.

The uncomfortable summary: **on this evidence the panel's machinery is doing a great deal of work to approximately reach what a single call already does.**

## The registered sensitivity analysis called this almost exactly

Appendix I item 3 registered `σ²_ba` at the ~60 pool as a **transported assumption** (2.3562, from the matched-N=30 fit × 1.949), shipped with its 95% interval and a sensitivity table across it. The achieved values:

| | Registered (point) | Registered (upper 95%) | **Achieved** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `σ²_ba` | 2.3562 | 7.7211 | **7.8266** |
| `σ²_e` | 4.7917 | 4.7917 | **3.6348** |
| SE at B=24/R=2 | 0.629 | 0.918 | **0.911** |
| MDE (Holm m=2) | 1.94 | 2.83 | **2.81** |

**Read the achieved column carefully — its three rows do not come from one calculation.** `σ²_ba` and `σ²_e` are the rarefied fit's own components (the residual computed as pooled within-(arm × brief) variance over the rarefied responses, df 70). The achieved **SE is read off the fitted `vcov`**, not computed from the SE formula: the formula assumes a balanced B=24/R=2, and this run is 141 unbalanced cells. Feeding the achieved components back through the registered formula gives **0.8965** against the fit's 0.9112 — a 2% gap, which is the imbalance, and the reason the fitted value is the one reported.

Spelling that out because the rows invite an arithmetic that does not work: `SE = sqrt(2·(7.8266/24 + 4.7917/48))` = 0.923, using the *registered* `σ²_e` against the *achieved* `σ²_ba`. That mixes columns. `σ²_e` also moved, and downward.

**The transport erred on the interaction term, not the residual.** `σ²_e` came in **below** its registered value (3.63 against 4.79 — rarefying a ~60 pool down to 45 reduces the within-cell variance, as it should), while `σ²_ba` came in **3.3× above** its transported point estimate. The ×1.949 pool-size scaling of the *residual* was sound; what it could not do was carry the brief × arm interaction, which is precisely the quantity Appendix I item 3 flagged as **not estimable from Stage 1a** and registered as an assumption.

The run landed within a hair of the registered interval's upper bound. Registering the assumption *with* its interval rather than as a point is what makes that a recorded outcome instead of an embarrassment — and it is the reason the achieved MDE of 2.81 was a pre-stated possibility rather than a surprise.

**Consequence for reading S1b-1:** |−1.77| sits below the achieved MDE of 2.81, so the contrast is not powered to *resolve* a difference of the size observed. The CI is the load-bearing statement, not the point estimate.

## The big caveat: the panel arm is truncation-limited

**This is the finding that most constrains what S1b-1 licenses, and it is not a result about panels.**

All three failures are `S1B-PANEL`, all `parse_failure` / `partial_truncated`, all on `sci-08`/`sci-09`. In each, one of five agents hit `max_tokens` mid-reply, contributed nothing, and the undersized pool was discarded rather than silently stored (issue #102 working as designed).

Those three are the visible tip. Across the **surviving** cells:

| Arm | Cells short of 60 raw candidates | Mean raw candidates |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `S1B-SOLO60` | **3 / 48** (6%) | 61.2 |
| `S1B-APRIME` | 6 / 48 (13%) | 59.3 |
| **`S1B-PANEL`** | **19 / 45 (42%)** | 57.8 |

**The panel arm bumps the output ceiling seven times more often than the solo arm it is being compared against.** Appendix F item 6 measured `max_tokens` per (model, effort) on *solo* runs; a panel's round-2 prompt carries the shared pool as context and its agents run longer. The ceiling was never sized for this arm shape.

Both known biases push the same way — **against the panel**:

1. Truncated agents contribute fewer ideas, so surviving panel cells are systematically short.
2. The three lost cells are the ones that truncated *worst*, i.e. plausibly the longest-output cells, so the loss is **not at random** and removes panel cells from the upper tail.

The panel is below solo in **every stratum** (aut 50.1 vs 54.2, biz 43.5 vs 51.8, prod 49.7 vs 52.8, sci 46.6 vs 54.3), so the direction is not an artifact of losing three `sci` cells. But the *magnitude* of S1b-1 is not trustworthy, and a correctly-sized ceiling could move it. **S1b-1 should be treated as "no advantage demonstrated, magnitude unresolved", not as a measured penalty.**

S1b-2 is far more robust: both arms are panels with identical geometry under the same ceiling, and a +14.91 effect is not an artifact of a 42%-vs-13% truncation difference.

## What this licenses, against what was registered

Appendix I item 5 pre-registered the interpretation of four outcomes. **The observed one — a negative point estimate with the advantage bounded at zero — was not among them**; item 5 enumerated a null, not a negative. Stating that plainly rather than retrofitting item 5's language:

- **`#131` (model comparison) stays blocked, and its rationale weakens further.** Ranking models through panel machinery that does not clear a single call ranks a difference the machinery has not earned.
- **`#132`'s factorial is now the right question, re-scoped.** Not "which panel configuration is best" but "is there *any* configuration that clears one call" — and S1b-2 says the persona lever is where the variance lives, so panel size, sharing and rounds are the factors to move against a fixed differentiated-persona baseline.
- **The `max_tokens` ceiling must be re-measured for panel arms before any of that.** Re-running S1b-1 under a correctly-sized ceiling is the cheapest way to convert "no advantage demonstrated" into a measured number, and it is a config change — new `armsConfigHash`, full re-collection — so it belongs in the same amendment as any `#132` design.

Unchanged: §5.1's judge gate has never run (`#16`), so all 276 judge calls collected here remain stored data, and every idea-level metric stays unreportable. The primary response above is rarefied `distinct_k` throughout.

## The registered H1–H5 family was not built, by design and now by construction

Appendix I item 4 registered that Stage 1b builds its own two-contrast family and **not** H1–H5. The analysis confirms it mechanically: all five slots report NOT ESTIMABLE with p = 1, keeping m = 5 for anything that later fits this store.

H1's refusal is worth quoting, because it is `#145`'s guard working on live data rather than in a test:

> H1 is registered as mean(panel arms) − A (registered set `prereg-2026-08-01`); this run's reference arm is `S1B-SOLO60`. A contrast against a different baseline is a different quantity, not a weaker H1, and must not occupy H1's Holm slot.

Before `#145`, H1 would have been **fully estimable** here — `mean(S1B-PANEL, S1B-APRIME) − S1B-SOLO60`, a number that averages a panel with its own ablation, wearing the registered name of the product's core claim. It would have read −9.22 and looked like a devastating result. It is not a result at all.

H5 is separately not identifiable: every generator arm here is Anthropic, so `sameProvider` is constant within each judge provider and the bias column is collinear with the intercept. Reported as not computed rather than fitted anyway.

## A harness defect this run surfaced

The registered analysis could not run at first: `buildFrame` treated the three **batch-replay resume records** as an arm called `__batch-replay__`, found it had zero completed rows, and threw `DifferentialAttritionError` — an error about attrition describing nothing that happened, on a healthy store.

Any batched run with at least one failed cell was unanalysable. Stage 1a never hit it because it ran `--no-batch`; fixing `#148` is what made batch mode usable and therefore what made this reachable. Filed as `#157`, fixed in `#158`.
