# Stage 1b, re-run: the panel loses to one call

**2026-09-09.** Store `results-study1b-r2`, configHash `07de3db9df06`, 431/432 cells, **$93.8517**, ~75 minutes.

Registered in advance by [Appendix J](PREREGISTRATION.md) (B=48, R=3, sized on measured variance components) and [Appendix K](PREREGISTRATION.md) (no batch mode). Reproduce with `node evals/analysis/stage1b.mjs --results-dir results-study1b-r2`.

## The result

| ID | Contrast | Estimate | SE | 95% CI | Holm p (m=2) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **S1b-1** | `S1B-PANEL − S1B-SOLO60` | **−2.1338** | 0.6311 | **[−3.371, −0.897]** | **0.0007** |
| **S1b-2** | `S1B-PANEL − S1B-APRIME` | +17.0833 | 0.6311 | [15.846, 18.320] | <0.0001 |

**At a matched pool size, a five-agent two-round panel produces significantly FEWER distinct ideas than a single solo call.** This is the finding the first run could not reach. It reported −1.7672 with Holm p = 0.052 — "no advantage demonstrated, magnitude unresolved." The re-run resolves it in the direction the point estimate already pointed, and the effect is slightly larger, not smaller.

The product's claim is that a differentiated panel beats one call. On the study's registered primary metric, at equal pool size, it does the opposite.

## The estimand

Rarefied `distinct_k` (Appendix C items 2–5), the registered primary. Full-pool is secondary descriptive.

| Arm | Rarefied (n=50) | Full pool | Mean cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `S1B-SOLO60` | **44.570** | 53.250 | $0.0456 |
| `S1B-PANEL` | **42.495** | 49.161 | $0.1920 |
| `S1B-APRIME` | **25.353** | 27.944 | $0.1504 |

The panel costs **4.2×** the solo arm and returns fewer distinct ideas. On the cost lane the gap is starker still: 256 distinct ideas per dollar against solo's 1168.

## S1b-2 is not a consolation

`S1B-APRIME` is the panel geometry with personas disabled — five agents, one neutral persona. It collapses to 25.4 rarefied, far below both. So the personas are doing real work *within the panel design*: +17.08, p<0.0001.

But that is a comparison between two panel configurations, and it says nothing about whether either beats one call. S1b-1 answers that, and the answer is no. **The persona machinery rescues the panel from being much worse; it does not make it better than the alternative it is sold against.**

## The sizing was right

Appendix J predicted SE **0.614** at B=48/R=3 from the achieved variance components. The realized SE is **0.6311** — within 3%.

That is worth stating plainly because the *first* run's sizing was badly wrong in the flattering direction: it assumed a transported `σ²_ba` of 2.3562 when the measured value at this pool scale is **7.8266**. Registering the design on a measured component instead of a transported one is what converted p=0.052 into p=0.0007.

## The ceiling fix worked

[Issue #160](https://github.com/Kromatic-Innovation/ideate-core-evals/issues/160) replaced a per-idea `max_tokens` rate with a per-request floor of 6873.

| Arm | short of 60 — first run | re-run | min pool |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `S1B-SOLO60` | 6% (3/48) | 12% | 51 |
| **`S1B-PANEL`** | **42% (19/45)** | **22%** | **50** |
| `S1B-APRIME` | 13% (6/48) | 10% | 55 |

Panel truncation halved, and — more important for the analysis — the *depth* of shortfall collapsed. The rarefaction floor rose from **45 to 50**, clearing Appendix J item 8's registered threshold of 48, so both contrasts are **primary as registered**. The floor was set by `arm=S1B-PANEL|brief=sci-03|rep=0`, which is reported rather than re-planned: re-planning the cell that sets the floor, because it sets the floor, would be selecting on the outcome.

## Failures

One cell: `S1B-PANEL|brief=biz-04|rep=1`, `parse_failure`, with **5 of 5 agents replying**. A malformed pool, not a truncation — exactly the format residual Appendix J item 3 registered as ceiling-independent, and at 1 in 432 it came in *better* than the ~4% per-reply baseline predicted. `parse_failure` is intrinsic, so it stays failed rather than re-planning.

Two transient `fetch failed` retries (one OpenAI, one Anthropic), both absorbed at attempt 1 of 4.

## What this does and does not license

**Does:** the panel, as configured here, is worse than one call at matched pool size on `distinct_k`. Appendix I item 5 registered in advance what a null would license; this is stronger than a null and points the other way.

**Does not:**

- **Idea-level quality is untested.** §5.1's judge gate has never run ([#16](https://github.com/Kromatic-Innovation/ideate-core-evals/issues/16)), so every idea-level metric stays unreportable. A panel that produces fewer but *better* ideas is not ruled out by this run — and cannot be, until the gate runs.
- **The panel arms are less differentiated than the shipped product.** §3.3 strips sampling parameters, so arms differ by stance and strategy only. This is a weaker panel than `DEFAULT_PERSONAS`.
- **H1–H5 remain NOT ESTIMABLE**, for [#145](https://github.com/Kromatic-Innovation/ideate-core-evals/issues/145)'s reason: these are not the registered panel arms, so no slot may be occupied by a differently-based contrast.

## Consequence for the issue graph

[#131](https://github.com/Kromatic-Innovation/ideate-core-evals/issues/131) (model comparison) and [#132](https://github.com/Kromatic-Innovation/ideate-core-evals/issues/132) (the four-factor process design) were both blocked on Stage 1b. They are now unblocked, but their premise has changed: ranking models inside a panel, or optimizing panel size and sharing, both assume the panel is worth running. **The measured baseline says it is not**, so the next study's first job is to find a panel configuration that clears one solo call — or to establish that quality, not count, is where the panel pays.

**#16 is now the binding constraint on the whole study.** Every remaining claim about the panel is a quality claim, and quality is exactly what has never been measured.
